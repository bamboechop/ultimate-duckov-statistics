# M17 retained Combat

Combat uses four native retained subpages: Summary, Enemies, Weapons & ammunition, and Incoming damage. `CombatPresentationFactory` copies UI values from one factory-bound `StatisticsPanelProjection`; `CombatView` owns Unity controls and consumes the immutable result. No tracking, persistence, schema, migration, or export contract changes are involved.

## Data and evidence

| Surface | Source and qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Overall | `Combat.Lifetime.Totals.DamageDealt`, `DamageReceived`, `KillsByYou`, and `PlayerDeaths`, each with its own current capability. Supported zero is zero. Retained positive values with incomplete evidence are labelled partial; unproven zero is unavailable. |
| Ranged and melee | Firing actions come from `Weapons.Lifetime.Totals.FiringActions`. Hits, swings, headshots and headshot final blows come from their Combat totals. Accuracy uses `CombatStatisticsViewModel.Accuracy`: unique damaging player projectiles divided by completed player projectiles, never firing actions. Ranged/melee kills use `PlayerKillPartition` from the same final-blow event. Nonzero Effect, Environmental and Unknown buckets remain separate. Current capability and repaired-data qualification remains. |
| Observed world deaths | `Combat.Lifetime.Ownership`, using only `ObservedWorldDeaths`. Other NPC, Environmental and Unknown remain visible, with Companion when nonzero. Player kill credit is excluded. |
| Enemies | `Combat.Enemies`: identity/display fallback, `Totals.DamageCaused`, `KillsByYou`, and `ObservedWorldDeaths`. Initial ordering is kills descending. Every header sorts its own name, damage, Int64 player-kill count or Int64 world-death count; repeated clicks reverse direction. Missing evidence stays last. Enemy and incoming sort state are independent. |
| Weapons | Union of firing/ammunition groups, exact recorded throwable uses and player-relevant `Combat.Lifetime.Weapons` records, joined only by ordinal exact stable weapon ID. Combat-only records require player damage, kills, completed player projectiles, hits, swings or headshots; incoming damage, player deaths and NPC/world-only evidence cannot populate this player weapon list. Melee-only weapons remain visible. Unknown player identities stay separate by source and never supply another weapon's counters or ammunition. Ordering remains firing actions descending, ordinal name and ID; overall share uses supported lifetime firing actions. Selection chooses the first deterministic row when its previous ID is absent. |
| Ammunition | Only the selected weapon's exact pairs. Each percentage is the existing `WeaponAmmunitionPairView.PercentageWithinObservedWeaponPairs`. Uncorrelated firing actions have no invented ammunition identity. Uncorrelated actions qualify the basis as observed correlated pairs; a weapon without pairs explicitly shows no correlated ammunition data. The localized firing-action footer remains visible. |
| Incoming damage | `Combat.Killers`, positive `DamageReceived` or `PlayerDeaths` rows only. The pinned Total row uses lifetime damage received and player deaths. Shares divide by supported lifetime damage received; zero has no ratio. Initial sort is damage descending. Each column header selects descending order; repeated activation reverses it. Sorting uses underlying names, damage, shares and Int64 death counts, with unavailable values last in either direction and ordinal name/ID tie-breakers. Attacker count and deadliest attacker require supported identity/death evidence. A zero-death complete history yields None; stable Unknown stays Unknown. Missing required evidence yields Unavailable. This is incoming harm, not player kill credit. |

There is **no persisted per-enemy ownership breakdown**. `CombatTableRow.OwnershipBreakdown` explicitly represents the absent cross dimension, so current enemy rows have no expansion button or chevron. The presentation control supports a supplied breakdown with a compact clickable header and separate white-on-translucent-black read-only metric rows; no native data is invented to populate it. Its chevron rotates the supported right-facing glyph rather than requesting an unavailable down glyph. Global ownership marginals never enter an individual enemy expansion. Enemy family, cause, ownership, attacker and weapon identity are not conflated.

## Layout and interaction

Desktop Combat disables outer vertical scrolling, matching Runs; only its content columns scroll. Narrow layouts retain bounded outer scrolling for the stacked panels. All shared retained scroll regions, including Records, explicitly use Clamped movement while retaining native sensitivity, inertia and deceleration. Their bounds cannot be bypassed by the movement mode on Duckov's shared prefab. A player-attributed weapon with no proven attack type uses damage or kills as its list subtitle instead of Actions: Unavailable; unknown combat-only identity rows omit that subtitle.

The selected weapon expands into the established read-only Combat metric rows. Proven ranged activity shows Firing actions, Hits, Headshots, Headshot final blows, Kills by you and Damage dealt, with the existing ammunition column. Proven melee activity shows Swings, Hits, Kills by you and Damage dealt; ammunition is Not applicable and headshots are omitted. An exact identity with both recorded activity types retains separate ranged/melee hit counters. An identity without firing, projectile, hit, swing, classified-kill or throwable evidence/native classification keeps its common counters and an Unavailable combat-type notice; the UI does not infer type from its name or icon. Damage uses `DamageDealt`, and kills use `KillsByYou`, never incoming damage or observed world deaths.

Each combat counter combines its own current capability with combat weapon-identity availability. Missing exact combat rows remain Unavailable. Repaired evidence and relevant unknown-weapon counters qualify retained values as partial; an unproven zero remains Unavailable. Firing actions also retain their own capability and missing-attribution qualification. Equipment → Weapons remains limited to equipped duration, slots and attachments. Throwable support adds native release tracking through the existing Item Use persistence and export contract; see [M17_THROWABLES.md](M17_THROWABLES.md).

The existing header, tabs, native font/material, reference transform and back control are reused. Combat starts 40 reference pixels below the header and ends 30 above the screen edge. Desktop selector and content widths have a 1:2 relation separated by 40 pixels. Outer surfaces are black at 50% opacity with 20-pixel corners and 30-pixel padding. Summary uses four equal cards and measured Ranged/Melee/world-death regions. Weapons and ammunition have independent columns. Native TMP measurement controls wrapping and row heights without altering horizontal font geometry. No blur or game image asset is shipped.

Below 1180 viewport pixels, the selector stacks above the page, summary sections follow reading order, and weapon/ammunition columns stack. Tables become labelled stacked rows when their content width cannot support four readable columns. A bounded outer region contains the stacked document; long page/column content has its own bounded viewport. The footer remains outside weapon scroll content.

Summary and Incoming damage cards place the white value above the smaller muted uppercase label. The inline observed-world total aligns using individual character quads with symmetric SDF padding removed. TMP textBounds uses face ascenders/descenders and cannot establish this visual alignment. The selected orange weapon uses white text for its name and both stat lines. Each table has one continuous rounded header band beneath transparent native header buttons. Document paint order is restored when pooled controls recycle. Table headers use 22-reference-pixel text against 28-pixel values; column widths reserve native measured room for either leading sort arrow before distributing remaining space. Both tables' header buttons remain usable in the narrow stacked layout. Sort selection persists through subpage changes and same-generation refresh, and resets on generation loss or replacement.

Each actionable selector, enemy and weapon row uses the shared `RunsHistoryButton`, full-row raycast background, native `ButtonAnimation`, native audio forwarding for selection/submit, and a separate interaction tint over persistent orange selection. Down from the main Combat tab enters the selector; Right enters the page, Left returns, and Up at the first selector returns to the tabs. Read-only pages accept controller scrolling in both directions. Focus reveals ancestors and stays associated with identity as controls recycle; wheel movement cancels pending row clicks.

All four subpages retain independent scroll/focus state. Weapon and ammunition scroll independently, with ammunition offsets retained per selected weapon. Valid same-generation refresh preserves selection, expansion and offsets, clamped to replacement extents. Missing or changed generation clears state and hides old content immediately. Visible-row pools materialize controls only for the viewport plus overscan, including large enemy, weapon, ammunition and attacker collections. Listeners are attached once per control and removed on disposal; runtime item icons are borrowed through `NativeItemIconResolver` with a deterministic question-mark fallback.

`NativeWeaponFireAdapter` captures ammunition `Item.TypeID` as `duckov:ammo:{TypeID}`. Combat resolves that same TypeID through the resolver's `duckov:item:` namespace, preserving the captured ammunition identity. Metadata identity validation and missing/modded fallback remain in the existing resolver; there is no name-to-image lookup or packaged native asset.

Rounded stencil masks supplement rectangular culling, covering TMP glyphs, fallback submeshes, shadows, icons, rows and selection backgrounds. The shared native ScrollRect configuration supplies sensitivity, inertia and nested edge propagation. Subtle white overflow cues follow the panel's rounded contour: no cues when fitted, bottom at the top endpoint, both in the middle, top at the bottom endpoint.

## Qualification boundary

The initial renderer had two lifecycle defects visible in user screenshots: its measurement GameObject stayed inactive beneath an initially inactive shell, and decorative rows disabled buttons while leaving their ColorTint overlays visible. Inspection of the installed native assemblies confirmed that `TextMeshProUGUI.Awake` establishes orthographic UI measurement (otherwise preferred metrics use the 0.1 world-text scale), and `Selectable.OnDisable` calls `InstantClearState`, tinting its target graphic white regardless of `disabledColor`.

`CombatNativeTextMeasurement` now keeps the scratch GameObject active and disables only its renderer. `RunsHistoryButton.BindInteractionOverlay` hides the separate overlay on decorative rows and restores the current native tint immediately on actionable rows, including recycled controls. `CombatNativeControlTests` executes these production adapters against narrow models of those installed lifecycle boundaries, including cold shell activation, reopening, multiline measurements, decorative/actionable reuse and focus tint. These tests cover the previous mechanisms but do not substitute for screenshots from Unity.

`RetainedCombatTests` exercises production presentation, generation binding/state, relational measured document composition, deterministic sorting and percentages, truthful empty/partial/unavailable states, scroll and focus policies, the linked production row button, pooled resource lifetime, long names and large collections. These game-independent tests do not execute Unity's renderer or certify audible feedback, real EventSystem raycast order, stencil rendering, or live GameObject counts. The installed native build and contract probe establish compilation and baseline contract compatibility.

Visual/audio/input/lifecycle acceptance remains user-controlled under [M17_MANUAL_VALIDATION.md](M17_MANUAL_VALIDATION.md). Text fit, pixel geometry and screenshot comparisons are never production construction or availability gates. This document does not claim deployment or gameplay qualification.
